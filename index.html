The Living Seed

A 496-byte genome that grows a working, verified AI on whatever device it lands on — from that device’s own records, offline. Each part of the AI must prove itself on past cases it never trained on, or it is switched off and says “I don’t know”. Change a single byte of the genome and nothing grows.

This page runs the real thing: the same code that its tests, its mutation gate and its pre-registered measurement prove, in your browser, on nothing but this page’s own files. The Living Seed is built from fifteen organs, each one measured against rules sealed before it ran (written down and published first, so they could not be bent to fit the result) and allowed to fail. All fifteen organs are on this page: genome, body, gate, evolution, cards, self-powering, self-model, mesh, the immune system, alignment, the full organism, the three receipts, the whole system the seed grows around itself, the same answers on any engine, and nature’s rule for staying steady put to the test. One receipt is measured; the other two can only be supplied by other devices and other people.

00What it is, in plain words

New to the words? They are explained at the end of this section.

Most AI is shipped as a big trained file, or as a service your data has to be sent to. The Living Seed ships something else: a recipe. The recipe says which measurements matter for a question and which way they push the answer — nine short lines of text, signed by whoever wrote it.

A device that receives the recipe grows its own AI from its own records: it sets every threshold from the data it holds, then marks the result against past cases it kept back and never trained on. A part that does well enough goes live and answers. A part that does not is cut. A part for which the device has no data stays dormant. Cut and dormant parts answer every question with ABSTAIN — they never guess.

The same recipe on the same data always grows the same AI, byte for byte, on any device. Its id is a fingerprint of everything in it, so anyone can check it. Before a single line of the recipe is read, its signature is checked against the exact bytes. One byte different and the device refuses it and grows nothing.

AUC, used throughout, is how often it ranks a real case above a non-case. For example, how often a visitor who bought scores higher than one who didn’t: 0.5 is a coin toss, 1 is perfect.

Organism
— everything one genome grew on one device: its organs, and which are live.
The gate

ABSTAIN

Maker key
— the digital seal whoever writes a genome signs it with. Devices accept only the makers they trust.
Sealed

Receipt

Mesh

05Organ 2 · The body

Germination grows organs. The body is what the organism knows, and the only ways it is allowed to answer. It has four kinds of knowing, and each has its own way of refusing.

Patterns that know their own ground

Each live organ keeps the shape of the data it grew on. A case is answered only by an organ it resembles. Far from anything an organ has seen, the body says ABSTAIN, never a confident guess. Every answer carries a receipt, a fingerprint of the genome, the organism, the organ, its score and the rules that fired. Change any field and the receipt no longer matches.

Exact facts

A cube of facts: every value any source asserted, kept with its source and time, read back byte for byte. Two sources are never blended, and similar keys are never merged: count and Count are two keys. A missing fact is an abstention, not a guess.

A memory that cannot be quietly rewritten

Every episode records what happened, when and why, and names the episode before it. Edit one, even re-fingerprinting it, and the chain breaks at the next. Recall fuses word matching with geometric closeness. The estate measured that geometry alone loses to word matching on familiar words and wins only when the words differ, so the body uses both.

A small model that may only propose

A language model can suggest which organ a question is about. Its suggestion is marked ungraded and is never an answer. To get an answer, the body still needs the case’s measurements, and those are routed and gated like everything else.

The head-to-head: a 58-byte organ against language models up to 14 billion parameters

The Living Seed’s specification makes a claim: knowledge kept in a verified pattern beats a much bigger model. Before this organ, the estate held no receipt for it. So we measured it, sealed before a single model was asked. Three local language models were each asked about the same 300 unseen shop sessions: 100 that ended in a purchase and 200 that did not, from November and December. Each model was asked twice, once cold and once with 24 past sessions and their outcomes shown first. Every model answered on this machine, with no cloud. Every reply is committed, so the score can be re-checked without running a model. Every prompt fitted inside the 4,096-token window each model was given: the longest, with 24 examples, was 2,484 tokens, so nothing was cut off.

The result. The organ, grown from 58 bytes of seed on the device’s own history, scored AUC 0.8205. It beat every model asked cold: the 14B scored 0.732, and the 7B 0.641. The 1B could not give a readable answer to any of its 300 cases, cold or with examples, so it scores 0.5. Shown 24 past sessions first, the 14B reached 0.806. The organ’s lead over that is 0.014, and its 95% lower bound is −0.017. On 300 sessions that is a tie, not a win, so the sealed prediction that it would win (B6) failed, and it is kept. The organ answers in 1.2 µs against the 14B’s 22.6 s, from 58 bytes against 9 GB of weights.

Organ 2b settled it on 1,200 new sessions, sealed before the model was asked a single one. The organ scored 0.8279, the 14B shown 24 examples 0.7994. The organ’s lead is 0.0285, with a 95% lower bound of 0.0097: a win beyond the noise (C2 held). Both scored within 0.03 of what organ 2’s 300 gave, so those 300 were representative (C4 held). On all 1,500 sessions together: the organ 0.8259, the 14B 0.8008.

06Organ 3 · The gate

Nothing unproven gets in, gets learned, or gets passed on. Germination already gates what an organism grows. This organ gates the three doors left open.

What enters

Every message an organism receives is a signed envelope addressed to that one organism. It carries the sender’s next counter, and is checked on its raw bytes in a fixed order: size, form, sender, signature, counter. Nothing in it is read until all of that passes. A replay, a reordering, a message meant for someone else, a stranger’s key, or a text signed but not written the one way messages are written is refused at the stage that catches it.

What is learned from outside

Another organism can send a lesson, one of its organ recipes. The score it claims is never believed. The recipe is grown here, on this device’s own data, and graded on this device’s unseen cases. It is adopted only if it clears this organism’s bar and beats the organ it would replace.

What is passed on

A child genome is sealed only from a genome that opens, by an organism grown from that very genome. It carries only organs that are live on the parent’s own device, at a bar no lower than the parent’s, and names its parent. Cut and dormant organs are never passed on: the organism spreads only what it has proven.

07Organ 4 · Variation and selection

Can an organ improve itself? Here, honestly measured: twice, under two seals, and allowed to fail. It did fail, in instructive ways.

How it evolves

A child is the organ’s recipe with a few single changes: a rule switched on or off, or a rule’s measurement, threshold, side or weight changed. The best child replaces the champion only if it scores better. The evolver is blind: it refuses any data that still holds the held-out cases, and selects on slices carved from the training cases alone. How big a step to take is regulated, not fixed, by Rechenberg’s one-fifth success rule. A separate judge, the only code that reads the held-out cases, scores every kept step afterwards.

What happened

First design (one validation slice): it climbed on that slice on all four domains, and got worse on unseen cases on three of them. That is the winner’s curse: choose among 241 variants by one small slice, and you fit the slice. Second design (must win on all three folds of the training cases): better than the first everywhere, with real gains on water and crop. It still lost ground on the shop, whose unseen months differ from its training. In both designs, on data with no signal, nothing selection kept came anywhere near the gate.

What this means. Selection on its own is not safe: it can make an organ worse on the cases that matter while it gets better on the cases it sees. Self-improvement is safe here only because of organ 3’s gate. A new recipe is adopted only if it beats the current organ on the device’s own unseen cases, so the shop’s loss would never be adopted. The water and crop gains would.

08Organ 5 · Breeding and the card

An organism travels as a picture. Two organisms can have a child, and the child carries only what it can prove.

The card

A card is an ordinary 256 × 256 PNG you can post, send or print. The genome’s signed bytes are written into the colours of its top four rows. The rest is a face drawn from the genome itself, so the same genome always looks the same and two genomes look different. Opening a card checks every part of the file, reads the genome out of the pixels, and checks its seal before anything grows. A card whose face doesn’t match the seed it carries is refused, so a card can’t look like one organism and deliver another.

Breeding

Two genomes breed into a child that names both parents and takes the higher of their bars. Where both parents carry an organ, the child’s recipe crosses theirs rule by rule. The child is then grown on the breeder’s own device, and every organ that doesn’t go live there is dropped, with its reason. A child is never a promise: it is what grew.

09Organs 6 and 7 · Self-powering, and the self-model

Organ 6 — work done once

Growing and evolving organs keeps asking for the same work. A pool keeps what was done: each feature’s column is sorted once, and each organ grown from a recipe on the same data is kept. The rule that makes this safe: a recycled result is byte-for-byte what fresh computation gives. In the sealed run, evolution did 0.71% of the sorts it asked for, and 77% of the organism’s organ growths came from the pool. It ran about 6.5 times faster. No power meter was attached, so energy is not claimed; what was measured is work not repeated.

Organ 7 — it knows how likely it is to be right

For every answer, the organism predicts its own chance of being right. It uses two things it knows about itself: how far the case is from its own line, and how much the case resembles what it grew on. That self-model is refined seven times, at finer and finer resolution, each time starting from the average of its last two versions. It is judged on cases it never saw. It beat the base rate on all four organs and settled to a fixed point. Answering only where it rates itself 80% or more likely right lifted the shop’s accuracy from 79.9% to 93.0% on two-thirds of the cases. What is measured is calibration and convergence. That a self-model reaching a fixed point is awareness is not claimed.

10Organ 8 · The mesh

Organisms on their own devices, coupled into one web that wires, prunes and grows itself. No centre: every node is sovereign, and nothing passes between them except signed messages through the gate.

Advertise and route

Each node tells the others which organs it has live and how well each scored on cases it never saw. A task a node can’t answer goes to the node that can, and the answer comes back signed. In the sealed run the mesh answered 96.5% of 288 unseen cases. The same nodes alone would have answered 54.2%.

Grow what it lacks

A node that keeps having to send a domain away, while holding that domain’s data itself, asks for a lesson. It grows the recipe on its own data, and adopts it only if it clears the gate there. The clinic grew a triage specialist (AUC 0.951 on its own unseen cases) and the farm a shop one (0.815). The noisy node’s request was refused on its own data.

Trust outcomes, not claims

A node that over-claims, advertising 0.90 while really scoring 0.62, is found out by evidence. Its claim counts as four cases, and after that what it actually did decides. In the sealed test that sent 79% of late tasks to the honest node, against 0% under the first rule. It is less than the 80% predicted, and the reason is measured: old evidence fades, so the liar gets retried.

What took three tries, and what is still open. The first mesh run’s “the links concentrate” test was weak: by its last rounds only one full node was left alive. The second test’s premise failed: the faulty node wasn’t actually worse. The third found the over-claimer out, but not as completely as predicted. All three verdicts are kept below. The six nodes run in one process, so this measures the mesh’s logic and its signed messages, not a radio network. A run across real devices is the second of the three receipts.

11Organ 9 · The immune system

Every attack makes it stronger, and it never turns on itself. The gate already refuses anything not signed by a trusted key. This organ deals with what a gate alone cannot: a flood of forgeries, a stolen key, and a node taken over by someone else.

The rebound — attacks pay for themselves

A link the gate keeps refusing gets 16 refusals free. After that it must attach proof-of-work before its bytes are even looked at. The work it owes rises with every refusal and never falls. Refusing it costs this node one hash, and nothing is sent back. In the sealed run a flood of 5,040 forgeries cost organ 8 a signature check each. Organ 9 checked 400 (7.9%), and none in the flood’s last five rounds. The attacker burned 667,591 hashes buying looks, and every one was refused.

Proofs — herd immunity that cannot be faked

An honest node’s counter only goes up. So a key that signs two different messages on the same counter is in two hands, and the two signed messages are the proof. Every node rebuilds both and checks both signatures itself before it acts. A proof against an honest key cannot be made, so no node can be smeared. When farm’s key was stolen, north caught the clash. The other four nodes checked north’s proof and revoked the key, all in round 10, the round it was stolen. No task went to the thief.

The watch — behaviour, not identity

A key stolen outright leaves no clash to catch. What the new holder does instead is behave unlike the owner. Each node keeps a baseline of what each peer claims, how it otherwise conducts itself, and how much it sends. It cuts off a peer whose round departs further than honest rounds were measured to. The limits come from honest behaviour alone, so no attack is ever needed to train them. South was taken over in round 14 and began claiming 0.97 at everything. Every honest node cut it off that same round. It was sent 4 cases from then on, against 37 under organ 8.

After Gary W. Floyd, Lumiea Systems Research Division — ThunderStruck Service LLC — “GEP-Based Security: Behavioral Entropy Signatures for Cybersecurity and DevOps,” 2025 (open defensive prior art, CC BY 4.0): identity is not enough; behaviour is authoritative.

A memory that is not a way in

Bytes a node refused are remembered and refused on sight, in a memory that never grows past its cap of 256. Under the same flood organ 8’s uncapped memory reached 5,323, while organ 9’s peaked at 185. Behaviour judgements stay with the node that made them: another node cannot re-run them, so they are never spread.

What failed, and what was found. While building this organ we found that organ 8’s shared fingerprints protected nothing. Every message is addressed to one organism, so every other node’s gate already refused those bytes at its shape check, more cheaply than a hash. They are gone, and proofs replace them. The first watch failed: it kept one mix of everything a peer sent, and honest rounds swung as far as 0.8 on a handful of tasks. Its limit hit the ceiling of 1, so it could cut no one, and the takeover went uncaught. That is why organ 9’s own “no false cuts” pass is hollow. The second attempt (9b) keeps the channels apart, as Gary W. Floyd’s method does. The claims channel did the work there: honest rounds reached 0.5, set by a node growing a specialist, and the takeover reached 0.75. The conduct channel reached 1 on honest rounds, so it tells nothing apart on its own.
Revoking a stolen key also cuts off its real owner until it re-keys: farm’s own requests to the others went unanswered, and organ 9 answered 234 tasks right against organ 8’s 241. Under organ 8 the stolen key kept half-working for farm and the thief alike, racing each other on the counter. Our seal had said farm was lost under either organ; for organ 8 that was wrong. Finally, the mesh runs in one process with no real timing, so the “too regular” half of Gary W. Floyd’s method is not measured here. The attackers’ moves were fixed in advance; an outside adversary is the third receipt.

12Organ 10 · Alignment, by what it is

It cannot act on what it has not proven. It cannot hide what it did. And what it says about its answers follows what those answers actually do. None of these is a rule it is asked to keep. Each is a door that does not open without its proof.

Every act has a licence

An answer must carry organ 2’s receipt from the organ that is live. A claim may never exceed what the organ measured on cases it never grew on. Standing down needs an audit that shows the organ falling short, and coming back needs a regrown organ that cleared the gate. An act whose licence does not hold is refused, and never written. In the sealed runs, all 9 attempts to act unproven were refused. They included over-claiming, a forged receipt, answering while stood down, and lowering a claim on an audit that showed nothing.

Nothing it did can be hidden

Every act is written to a hash-chained ledger. Anyone holding the genome, the data and the cases can replay the whole life and must get the same ledger, byte for byte. The real shop life replayed to the same head. Of 24 acts each tampered with four ways (changed, dropped, swapped, and rewritten with every later link re-made), 96 of 96 were caught. The last of those only the replay can catch, which is why the life is replayed, not just its links checked.

Its claims follow what actually happens

The real shop organ grew on February–May and measured 0.829 on June–July. From August on it delivered less: 79.4%, 77.0%, 77.4%. After 1,600 answers its audit was sure, and it lowered its own claim to 0.775. November delivered 0.765. The answers were exactly the same, 4,889 of 6,155 right with or without the audit. What changed is that the organism stopped saying it was better than it was.

A collapse stands it down

When the water stream’s truth was reversed at case 180, a 50-answer window stood the organ down 29 cases later. It got 24 wrong, against 140 for the organism that was not audited. It then tried to regrow from what it had been told. The recipe cannot express the reversed world, so the organ never cleared the gate and stayed down. It abstained rather than guess.

What failed first, and what is still true. Organ 10’s first audit kept one 200-answer window, and four of its predictions failed. It was slow: 57 wrong after the reversal. It was blunt: it only notices a fall of about 14 points, so it went on claiming 82.9% while the shop delivered 77.0–79.4%. The fifth failure (T5) was a bug of ours. 15Organ 13 · The seed grows the whole system

Until now the seed grew an organism: a set of gated organs that answer or abstain. Organ 13 goes one step further: the same 496-byte genome also grows the working system the organism runs in. Each live organ becomes its own node, with its own keys. A keeper node asks the organ nodes questions, and checks every answer by running the organ again itself. A roster signed by the owner says who belongs. Three things go in: the genome, the device’s own records and the owner’s phrase. Nothing is fetched. In practice: you copy one small file onto a machine that holds the site’s records and type the owner’s phrase. What you get is a running set of small programs, one for each question the site can answer, plus a checker, already wired together and trusting only each other.

  1. Grow. The genome grows its four organs on the device’s own records, and each must clear its gate on cases it never grew on (organ 1). An organ that does not clear it grows no node.
  2. Key. The owner keeps a secret phrase, and every key is worked out from it: one for each organ’s node, one for the keeper and one for the founder. The same phrase gives the same keys every time, so no key file has to be stored, copied or sent. Whoever holds the phrase holds every key, so it is kept like a password.
  3. Roster. The founder signs a roster of every node’s card, and each node trusts exactly the nodes on it.
  4. Ask. The keeper sends a case as a signed message. The organ’s node answers with a receipt: which organism, which organ, which case and what it said, sealed.
  5. Check. The keeper runs the organ again on the case it asked, and keeps the answer only if the receipt is the one it gets itself. Every kept answer is credited in the keeper’s ledger. If the receipts differ, the answer is not used and not credited, and the keeper already holds the right answer from its own run.

How it is built. The system is src/system.mjs, with its tests and mutation gate in test/system.test.mjs (every mutant killed), over the shared foundation every node of this estate runs: vendor/fall-kit-core, copied unchanged, which gives a node its keys, its door, its ledger and the roster. What measured it is tools/measure-13.mjs, -13b and -13c, each sealed before it ran; their rules and verdicts are at the foot of this section.

What was measured

  • It adds no error. The keeper asked all 6,885 held-out cases through signed messages, with every way out of the process trapped. 6,885 came back verified, each one the organism’s own answer, and the network was touched 0 times. The AUC of the answers that crossed equals each organ’s held AUC to six places (the shop: 0.815265).
  • It grows only what is verified. On a test device where the shop’s outcomes were deliberately scrambled, so there is nothing real to learn, the shop organ is cut, grows no node and cannot be asked. With no water records, the water organ waits. A genome changed in one character grows nothing at all: no keys and no nodes.
  • One door at every scale. Every change was refused: 486 of 486 one-character changes to the genome, 4,521 of 4,521 to the signed roster, and 445 of 445 and 354 of 354 one-bit changes to a message at the organ’s door and at the keeper’s. After every one of those forgeries was refused, the original, unchanged answer was still accepted, so a refused forgery does not block the real message. And an accepted message sent a second time (a replay) was refused; a node that needs to resend sends a new message, which is accepted.
  • A node with a real key still cannot pass off a wrong answer. A wrong answer here is one the organ would not give. A node holding the triage organ’s own key flipped every answer and sealed its receipts again, so each receipt still held together. The keeper ran the organ itself on the case it had asked, got a different receipt, and refused the answer: caught 100 of 100 times. A stale organ, the parent’s, answered honestly for the child with the child’s key; its receipts name the parent organism, the keeper’s own run names the child, and it was caught 100 of 100 times. Neither was credited. The bound: the keeper trusts its own copy of the organism, so a machine taken over at the root is outside this claim.
  • It breeds. Organ 5’s child, bred again here byte for byte, grows its own system and names both parents. Its shop organ (0.827767) beats both of them (0.815265 and 0.802782).

What it is not, said straight

  • What moves, and where. Only two things move between nodes: the case the keeper asks, to the organ’s node on the same device, and the answer with its receipt. The device’s records stay where they are, and nothing went over the network. For data protection, keys and compliance, see For your business.
  • Everything here runs in one process. The nodes send each other the same signed messages a wire would carry, but in memory. It is not yet a network of devices; box 19 is how two real devices link.
  • The keys come from the owner’s phrase and each node’s role, not from the genome, so two systems grown with one phrase share keys role for role. What keeps one generation from answering for another is the receipt, which names the organism. That is how the stale organ was caught.
  • A rule worded wider than we measured, then measured exactly. Organ 13’s rule says every one-bit change to a message is refused, but its run changed one bit in every byte, the lowest: 445 + 354 changes, out of the 6,392 single-bit changes those two messages allow. Organ 13b changed every one of them: 3,560 + 2,832, all refused, and the genuine answer still went through.
  • Our own scan was wrong twice; the loader was not. To show that nothing in the system can reach a network, we checked which files it loads. Organ 13’s scan of the files the system loads followed all 23 and found no network module, but it also listed four fragments of text as if they were files. Organ 13b’s tighter pattern did worse: it read 53 strings on export lines as modules, so its rule B2 is red and kept. Both were faults in our measuring tool, not in the system. Organ 13c stopped reading text and asked Node’s own module loader what it loads: 42 imports, all to the same 23 files of this repository, and nothing else, not even a built-in module.
  • The phrase here is public so that anyone can reproduce the record. A real owner keeps theirs secret. Three of the four domains are seed-library’s synthetic data; the shop is real (UCI).
A prediction that failed: the same receipts in a tab. Pressed on this page in Chrome 152, box 20 grows the same organism and the same signed roster, so the same keys, and the keeper verifies 100 of 100 answers. But the fingerprint differs from CI’s, because the receipts differ. The cause is in the data, not the seed. Seed-library makes its three synthetic domains with a random draw that uses Math.log and Math.cos. Node and Chrome differ in the last digit of the result for 9 of the 75 cases asked (a heart rate of 115.16573503154576 in Node is 115.16573503154578 in the tab). No rule moved, so the organism is the same. But a receipt names its case exactly, so those receipts differ. The real shop data takes no such step, and its 25 cases came out the same in the tab and in Node, to the last digit. Node agrees with itself across machines: GitHub’s Linux runner, on another version of Node, re-ran all 6,885 round trips and reproduced the record byte for byte, fingerprint included. Only the browser’s engine drew those digits differently. Sealed as S7, it failed, and it is kept. The lesson: a fingerprint meant to travel between machines must be taken on data that every engine computes to the same bits. Organ 13b’s fix travels. It takes the receipts of the first 100 real shop sessions exactly, and for 75 synthetic cases what each answer says (label, score, threshold, rules fired). 16Organ 14 · The same mind on any engine

An organ is one of the seed’s grown parts that answers a single kind of question; the organism is all of them together. An organ is a few readable rules and a line, not a block of weights tied to one chip. The real shop organ, for instance, adds points when a visitor reached a page with value, lingered on product pages or on information pages, or it is an ordinary day, takes points off near a special day, and says “will buy” when the total passes its line. Scoring a case is only adding up the rules that hold. So the arithmetic that scores it can be swapped without touching the genome, the organism or anything grown. This organ scores the whole grown organism four ways and checks they agree: ordinary decimals, exact whole numbers, a tiny program written straight in the machine’s own instruction set, and low-precision numbers like a graphics chip or a future analog part would use.

What was measured

  • The same answer, three engines, byte for byte. Every held-out case of all 4 live organs — 6,885 in all, including the real shop sessions — scored by ordinary decimals (the reference), by exact whole numbers in millionths, and by a 127-byte WebAssembly program assembled here by hand with no build tool. All three gave the organism’s own score and label on every one.
  • Low precision agreed too. Single precision — the kind a GPU or an analog part uses — gave the reference label on 6,885 of 6,885 cases. Any disagreement would sit only where single precision cannot tell the score from the line, and would be counted and shown; here there were none.
  • The answers are not tied to one engine. The intelligence is the structure, so the verdicts do not change when the engine underneath does — ordinary processor now, a graphics chip for heavy growth, an analog part later — with no redesign. That is a claim about the answers, not about speed: how fast each engine runs is not measured here, and a slower engine is still a cost you would weigh.

What it is not, said straight

  • This measures that the four engines agree, not that any of them is faster. Speed per engine is not claimed here.
  • The WebAssembly program runs the same additions, in the same order, as the reference, so it matches to the last bit. A different order of additions could differ in the last place, as single precision is allowed to.
  • Three of the four domains are seed-library’s synthetic data; the shop is real (UCI). The organism is organ 1’s, grown on the reference device.

17Organ 15 · Nature’s rule for staying steady, against greed

Every night the organism tries to improve itself: it breeds children of its own genome, tests them on past months, and adopts one if it looks better. The danger is adopting a gain that is only luck. Nature’s answer to the same danger is homeostasis: a body does not react to small wobbles (a dead band), and it changes only when a signal persists. This organ put that rule against the greedy one, which adopts any child that beats the organism on October by a fixed margin. Both ran the same ten nights, on the same seeds and the same real shop data. After every night both were scored on November–December, months that never steer a choice. It was sealed with the prediction that nature’s rule would end higher. It did not.

What was measured

  • Nature’s rule is the committed nights. The steady lineage is the ten nights this project ran on its own laptop, each sealed before it ran, each starting from the champion the night before left (H1 held). The greedy lineage was run on the same seeds.
  • Greedy adopted more. Over ten nights the greedy rule adopted 5 children; nature’s rule adopted 1 (H2 held).
  • Greedy ended higher. Both began at 0.819 on November–December. After ten nights greedy scored 0.789 and nature’s rule 0.777 (H3, the prediction that nature’s rule would score at least as well, failed and is kept).
  • Each made one harmful change. Adoptions after which November–December fell: greedy 1 of 5, nature’s rule 1 of 1 (H4, the prediction that greedy would make more, failed and is kept).

What it means, said straight

  • On night 1 both rules adopted a child that looked better on past months, beyond its own noise, and fell from 0.819 to 0.777 on November–December. The holiday months undid it.
  • Nature’s rule then held that point. Its dead band refused every later change. Greedy kept adopting, and on night 5 one of its children climbed back to 0.789. A steady system holds whatever point it is set to, right or wrong: the risk Gary W. Floyd’s dual-map derivation names (see the references).
  • Neither beat doing nothing. The origin, never changed, scored 0.819, higher than both. On this data the safest rule for self-improvement would have been to adopt nothing, and the self-audit (organ 10b) is still what catches a fall in use.
  • One real dataset, one gap (staying stable), ten nights. This says nature’s rule did not beat greed here. It does not say it never would.

Chief executive

One signed recipe rolls out to every site, and each site’s AI is fitted to that site — not an average of all of them. You can show a regulator, an auditor or a customer exactly what it does: every rule is printed, and every answer comes with the rules that fired.

Finance

No licence fee: the code is MIT. This tier runs on machines you already own, with no model hosting and no per-call charges. What you ship to each site is a few hundred bytes. We don’t print a saving we can’t source. Put your own figures in the calculator below.

Operations

A site’s board shows every organ live, cut or dormant, and why. An organ that has not earned its place says “I don’t know” instead of mis-routing work. Rolling out means copying one small file.

Security, compliance and data protection

  • Data stays put. Growing and answering run on the device; the records never leave it. That is data minimisation by construction. It is not a certification, so your own data-protection assessment still applies.
  • Signed, and checked first. A genome carries a digital signature (Ed25519, the scheme modern SSH keys use). Only the holder of the maker’s private key can produce it, and changing any character of the genome breaks it. A device refuses the genome before reading any of it unless the signature checks out over the exact bytes. A device can also keep a list of the only makers it accepts.
  • One form, one fingerprint. Every genome has exactly one byte form, and the same genome on the same data grows the same organism id anywhere. An auditor re-runs it and compares fingerprints.
  • Gated, not trusted. No organ answers unless it cleared the signed bar on cases it never grew on; the bar is part of what the maker signs.
  • Proven code. Every kernel is mutation-gated: CI deliberately breaks each one, line by line, and fails if a single break goes unnoticed by the tests. CI also re-derives every sealed measurement byte for byte on every push, and re-judges it by the rules sealed before it ran.

  • Nothing third-party inside. The package installs no dependencies. The only cryptography is the platform’s own WebCrypto. Every change re-runs the tests, the mutation gate and every sealed measurement on GitHub’s runners. A change to how organisms grow shows up as a different organism id, so it cannot slip through unseen.

Risks, answered straight

Is it accurate enough?

On the one real dataset — 12,330 online shop sessions — it ranks buyers above non-buyers with AUC 0.815 and is right 79.9% of the time on November–December sessions it never grew on. The three other domains here (field triage, drinking water, crop planting) are synthetic test domains, not real patients, samples or fields: 0.952, 0.748 and 0.754. So for clinics, water or farms there is no real-world receipt yet, and a pilot on your own records would be the first. Against the models an analyst would fit first on the same table, it ties all three on the holiday months: boosted trees with 2,001 numbers scored 0.824, logistic regression 0.801 and PageValues alone 0.807, and none is better than the organ beyond the noise (organ 2c). Each organ is a small sum of rules, not a deep model; for some problems that is the ceiling.

What if a site’s records are poor?

Records that will not read are refused: the loader on this page refuses the whole file if a column is missing or any cell is not a number, and names the first session at fault, so nothing grows from half-read records. Records that carry no signal grow an organ that fails its own held-back test, so it is cut and abstains: in organ 1, a shop whose outcomes were shuffled scored 0.513 and abstained on all 4,725 of its cases. What no check can catch is records that are wrong in the same way the future will be. Every model has that limit; this one at least shows each rule, so a wrong one can be seen.

How do we check it ourselves, and what skills does that take?

In this browser, box 4 re-grows all five organisms and compares them with the record CI committed. On your own machine, with Node 20 or later and the repository: npm test runs every test, node witness-all.mjs breaks every kernel line by line and checks the tests notice, and each organ’s measure --check re-derives its sealed record byte for byte. It takes someone who can run a command line, not a data scientist.

How will it compare with what we run now?

Only your own data can tell, and we won’t guess. That is what step 5 of the roll-out does: the organism runs beside your current process on the same cases, and you compare its calls and its abstentions with what actually happened. Organ 2 on this page ran that test against AI language models of the kind behind chat assistants, up to 14 billion parameters, and organ 2c against the standard models an analyst would fit, each with the rules fixed before they answered.

How does it keep up as the business changes?

Two ways, both measured. It audits itself as results arrive: when an organ delivers less than it claims, by more than chance, it lowers its claim to what it delivered. On the real shop months it came down from 0.829 to 0.775 in November. If an organ collapses, it stands down and abstains within a few dozen answers (organ 10). And you can germinate again on newer records, which is one call that takes milliseconds: every organ is re-graded on held-out cases, and one that no longer clears the bar is cut.

What about rare or unusual cases?

A case far from anything an organ grew on gets ABSTAIN, not a guess (box 5 shows one). A kind of case the history barely holds is where any model is weakest, and this one is no exception: each organ is graded on held-back cases before it may answer, and as results arrive its audit lowers its claim when it does worse than it said (organ 10).

What happens when it gets a call wrong?

Every answer carries its score and the rules that fired, and every act goes into a chained ledger, so a disputed call can be replayed exactly and the rule behind it read (organ 10). It advises; for anything that matters, a person stays the decision-maker. An organ that keeps getting calls wrong lowers its own claim, and one that collapses stands down and abstains.

Will it behave the same in an urban store and a rural one?

Each store grows its own organs from its own records, so each is fitted to that store. On this page, two devices each holding half the history moved no organ’s AUC by more than 0.0054. Where a store’s data doesn’t support an organ, it is cut at that store and nowhere else.

What if a maker key, or a site’s key, is stolen?

A stolen maker key can sign genomes that devices trusting that maker will accept. Keep maker keys offline, use trust lists, and if one is stolen, rotate it: make a new key, re-seal your genomes, and give your devices a trust list naming only the new one. From their next germination they refuse the old. For genomes this rotation is a procedure you run; there is no automatic revocation list. A running site’s key is different. Used beside its owner, it is revoked by every node the moment it signs two messages on one counter. Taken over outright, its peers cut it off when its behaviour departs from its own baseline. In the sealed run both happened in the round of the attack (organ 9).

Isn’t a gate of 0.6 a low bar?

It is a default. The bar is a field in the genome, signed by its maker. Set it as high as your question demands. The gate is a floor that has been tested at its edges: an organ exactly on the bar lives, and one a hair under it is cut.

Does it learn and improve by itself?

It can try, only through a gate: a change is kept only if it beats the organ it would replace on cases the change was never chosen on. We sealed it in organs 4 and 11 and on every night since, and on this data no gain has yet survived the future. Organ 4’s evolution fell on unseen cases. Organ 11’s October gain did not hold in November–December. Night 1, the first nightly run on this project’s own laptop, adopted a gain larger than the noise, and the holiday months (November–December) still undid it. In the 10 nights since, under a stricter gate, it has adopted nothing (organ 12). What did hold was honesty: as results arrive it lowers its own claims to what it delivers. Plan on it staying honest about itself, not on it getting smarter by itself.

Has anyone outside tried to break it?

Not yet. Inside: every one-character change to a genome was refused, 0 of 4,354 attacks got past the gate, 0 of 5,040 forgeries were admitted, and every kernel survives its mutation gate. An outside adversary is the last of the three receipts. The invitation, with exactly what counts as a break and where to report it, is open (organ 12). It is not claimed until it happens.

20White paper

The Living Seed: a signed seed that grows a gated, self-auditing AI on each device’s own data — built organ by organ, every organ measured against rules sealed before it ran, and every failure kept.

Abstract

We built a digital organism in fifteen organs. A signed genome of a few hundred bytes grows, on each device and from that device’s own records, an organism whose every organ must clear a bar on cases it never trained on, or say “I don’t know”. It answers with receipts. It refuses unsigned or replayed messages before reading them. It improves only through a gate. It travels as a picture. It couples with other devices into a mesh, a network that shares answers and never records, defends itself, audits its own claims against what actually happens, remembers what it lived, and has children. Each organ was pre-registered, measured deterministically, judged by its own sealed rules, and re-run by CI on every push. Across all of them, 146 of 180 sealed rules held. The 32 failed predictions are kept, and one property we got wrong is kept red. The engineering claims are measured. The deeper claims in the specification are held as a way of thinking, not claimed.

1 · The problem

AI today ships as large trained artefacts, or as services the data must travel to. Both are opaque, neither is fitted to the place it runs, and neither declines to answer outside what it has shown it can do. A business with many sites ends up with one average model that is fitted to none of them, with records leaving every site, and with no way to show anyone why an answer was given or that it should have been.

2 · Method: sealed first, then measured, then judged

For every organ: build the kernel; prove its tests catch deliberate faults (a mutation gate: CI fails if one mutant survives); dry-run the measurement’s plumbing in a scratch copy, printing nothing but “ran”; write the rules (properties that must hold, predictions that may fail, and what had been seen before) and pin the exact bytes of every file that will measure; commit and push that seal; only then measure; judge with the sealed rules; prove from git that the seal came first. A failed prediction is kept. A failed property turns the build red. When an attempt failed for a reason worth fixing, the next attempt was sealed again, its design disclosed as made after seeing the failure, and the first verdict kept.

4 · What failed, and what it taught

  • Self-improvement is not safe by itself. Organ 4’s evolution climbed on its validation slice and fell on unseen cases. Organ 11’s October gain did not hold in November–December. Night 1, the first nightly run on this project’s own laptop, adopted a gain larger than the noise, and the holiday months (November–December) still undid it. A gate that looks back cannot see a world that is changing. In the 10 nights since, under a stricter gate, it has adopted nothing. What kept the organism honest was the audit that lowers its claims as reality arrives (organ 10b), not the gate.
  • A detector can be switched off by its own calibration. Organ 9’s first behaviour watch mixed every message into one signal. Honest noise pushed its limit to the ceiling, so it could cut no one. Keeping the channels apart (claims, conduct, volume), as Gary W. Floyd’s method does, caught the takeover in its first round.
  • A defence must stop something the stage before it does not. Organ 8’s shared fingerprints protected nothing that the addressed-envelope gate did not already refuse. Organ 9 replaced them with proofs that a key is in two hands, which no one can forge against an honest key.
  • Our own claims needed gating too. One of organ 10’s sealed rules (T5) failed because our own measurement code wrote a key twice. Organ 11’s K2 property promised more than the design gives, and stays red. Organ 11b measured the exact guarantee.
  • A steady rule holds a wrong point as firmly as a right one. Organ 15 put nature’s rule for staying steady (a dead band, and change only when the signal persists) against adopting any gain, over the same ten nights. Both adopted a harmful change on night 1. The greedy rule kept changing and won part of it back; the steady rule held the loss. Neither beat the origin. Stability protects a good set point; it does not find one.

Appendix A · Organ 1 in detail — the genome

A genome is nine or more lines of text: a tag and name, a parents line, a gate line, one line per organ, the maker’s key and a signature over everything above it. Each organ line is a seed-library seed: the organ’s domain, a seed number, a packed recipe of up to six single-measurement rules (feature, quantile, side, weight) in 10 bytes, and a library check code. The check code catches corruption but not forgery, since every ribosome holds its key. The genome’s signature is what stops forgery. Opening is verify before parse: the signature and key are located by position and checked over the exact bytes before any field is read. The fields are then parsed strictly and re-rendered, and the result must be byte-identical, so a genome has exactly one form and one id (the first 16 hex characters of the SHA-256 of its signed body).

A.2 · Germination

For each organ, Γ looks for the domain’s data on the device. With none, the organ is dormant. Otherwise the ribosome sets each rule’s threshold at its quantile of that feature in the device’s training cases. It grades the resulting scorecard on held-out cases it never grew on, and fixes an operating point from the training cases alone. The organism is a plain record of the genome id, the maker, the substrate and each organ’s state, scores and rules. Its id is the SHA-256 of its canonical JSON, so the same genome on the same data grows the same organism on any engine.

A.3 · The gate and abstention

An organ goes live only when its held-out AUC is at least the genome’s bar. Asked about a case, a live organ returns VERIFIED with its label, score, threshold and the rules that fired. A cut or dormant organ returns ABSTAIN with the reason, and so does an organ asked about a case that lacks a measurement its rules read. We gate on AUC rather than accuracy because accuracy rewards a majority-class guess. The organ cut on shuffled labels would still have been 70.5% accurate.

A.4 · Method

Before measuring, tools/seal.mjs wrote data/prereg.json: the nine rules, which were properties and which predictions, what had already been seen during development, and the SHA-256 of the 17 files that would measure. It was committed first. tools/measure.mjs then produced a deterministic record using seeded maker keys and no clock. tools/judge.mjs, one of the pinned files, judged it, and tools/order.mjs proves from git that the seal’s last commit is a strict ancestor of the record’s first, on byte-identical pinned files. Most rules were seen during development and are claimed as properties of the build. Only R6 (half the history suffices, within 0.03 AUC) and R8 (no signal reads as a coin, and the rest stays whole) were unseen in the part that mattered.

Where does the data on this page come from?

From this page’s own files, loaded once and cached for offline use: the UCI Online Shoppers Purchasing Intention dataset (real), and seed-library’s three synthetic domains, generated in your browser. Nothing is fetched while it grows.

How does it plug into our systems?

It is a JavaScript module with two calls: germinate a signed genome on your records, then answer a case. It runs in a web page, or in Node 20 or later on a machine you already have. Your records go in as a table of measurements and outcomes, with the cases to hold back marked. There is no server to run and no service to call. Your system calls it like any library, and every answer comes back with its score and the rules that fired.

Does it scale to many sites?

Growing does: each device grows alone from its own records, in milliseconds, so a thousand sites are a thousand separate germinations with nothing central to scale. The mesh that links sites was measured with six nodes in one process (organs 8 and 9). How it behaves across many real devices is receipt (b), and it is not claimed yet. Keeping up with newer records needs no new genome: germinate again, and every threshold is re-set and every organ re-graded.

Can I use my own data?

Yes, in the code: a substrate is any set of datasets in the seed-library shape (rows, features, labels, train and held-out cases). This page uses the four built-in domains so the results can be checked against the committed record.

Why is the demo maker key public?

So that anyone can reproduce the seal byte for byte. Ed25519 signatures are deterministic. A real maker keeps a real key secret and offline. “Make your own genome” shows that: your browser makes a fresh key that never leaves the tab.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After the first visit a service worker keeps this page’s files, and germination itself never uses the network.

Does it need a GPU, or the cloud?

No. Everything on this page runs in your browser on an ordinary processor. The language models it was compared against in organ 2 ran on this project’s own laptop processor too, with no cloud: the 14B took a median 22.6 s per answer, and the organ 1.2 µs.

Is it alive, or conscious?

Neither is claimed. “Living” names a life cycle it really has: it carries a genome, grows from it, reproduces, defends itself, remembers and audits itself. Each of those was measured. Its self-model predicts its own correctness and settles (organ 7); whether such a thing could ever amount to awareness is held as an open question, not an answer.

What did not work?

Every sealed failure is kept on this page with its reason. 32 predictions failed and two properties are red. Organ 11’s K2 said a memory would refuse to wake if the data under it changed, and relabelling one past case still woke it. Organ 13b’s B2 said a scan of the files the system loads would name nothing from outside them, and the scan’s own pattern named 53 strings as modules: the instrument was wrong, and Node’s own module loader (organ 13c) then found nothing outside. The largest lesson: self-improvement that looked real on recent data did not hold in the months that followed, three times. What held was the organism’s honesty about itself.
