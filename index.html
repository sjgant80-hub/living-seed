The Living Seed

A 496-byte genome that grows a working, verified AI on whatever device it lands on — from that device’s own records, offline. Each part of the AI must prove itself on past cases it never trained on, or it is switched off and says “I don’t know”. Change a single byte of the genome and nothing grows.

This page runs the real thing: the same code that its tests, its mutation gate and its pre-registered measurement prove, in your browser, on nothing but this page’s own files. The Living Seed is built from twelve organs, each one measured against rules sealed before it ran and allowed to fail. Organs 1 to 9 are on this page: genome, body, gate, evolution, cards, self-powering, self-model, mesh and the immune system.

00What it is, in plain words

Most AI is shipped as a big trained file, or as a service your data has to be sent to. The Living Seed ships something else: a recipe. The recipe says which measurements matter for a question and which way they push the answer — nine short lines of text, signed by whoever wrote it.

A device that receives the recipe grows its own AI from its own records: it sets every threshold from the data it holds, then marks the result against past cases it kept back and never trained on. A part that does well enough goes live and answers. A part that does not is cut. A part for which the device has no data stays dormant. Cut and dormant parts answer every question with ABSTAIN — they never guess.

The same recipe on the same data always grows the same AI, byte for byte, on any device. Its id is a fingerprint of everything in it, so anyone can check it. Before a single line of the recipe is read, its signature is checked against the exact bytes. One byte different and the device refuses it and grows nothing.

AUC, used throughout, is how often it ranks a real case above a non-case. For example, how often a visitor who bought scores higher than one who didn’t: 0.5 is a coin toss, 1 is perfect.

05Organ 2 · The body

Germination grows organs. The body is what the organism knows, and the only ways it is allowed to answer. It has four kinds of knowing, and each has its own way of refusing.

Patterns that know their own ground

Each live organ keeps the shape of the data it grew on. A case is answered only by an organ it resembles. Far from anything an organ has seen, the body says ABSTAIN, never a confident guess. Every answer carries a receipt, a fingerprint of the genome, the organism, the organ, its score and the rules that fired. Change any field and the receipt no longer matches.

Exact facts

A cube of facts: every value any source asserted, kept with its source and time, read back byte for byte. Two sources are never blended, and similar keys are never merged: count and Count are two keys. A missing fact is an abstention, not a guess.

A memory that cannot be quietly rewritten

Every episode records what happened, when and why, and names the episode before it. Edit one, even re-fingerprinting it, and the chain breaks at the next. Recall fuses word matching with geometric closeness. The estate measured that geometry alone loses to word matching on familiar words and wins only when the words differ, so the body uses both.

A small model that may only propose

A language model can suggest which organ a question is about. Its suggestion is marked ungraded and is never an answer. To get an answer, the body still needs the case’s measurements, and those are routed and gated like everything else.

06Organ 3 · The gate

Nothing unproven gets in, gets learned, or gets passed on. Germination already gates what an organism grows. This organ gates the three doors left open.

What enters

Every message an organism receives is a signed envelope addressed to that one organism. It carries the sender’s next counter, and is checked on its raw bytes in a fixed order: size, form, sender, signature, counter. Nothing in it is read until all of that passes. A replay, a reordering, a message meant for someone else, a stranger’s key, or a text signed but not written the one way messages are written is refused at the stage that catches it.

What is learned from outside

Another organism can send a lesson, one of its organ recipes. The score it claims is never believed. The recipe is grown here, on this device’s own data, and graded on this device’s unseen cases. It is adopted only if it clears this organism’s bar and beats the organ it would replace.

What is passed on

A child genome is sealed only from a genome that opens, by an organism grown from that very genome. It carries only organs that are live on the parent’s own device, at a bar no lower than the parent’s, and names its parent. Cut and dormant organs are never passed on: the organism spreads only what it has proven.

07Organ 4 · Variation and selection

Can an organ improve itself? Here, honestly measured: twice, under two seals, and allowed to fail. It did fail, in instructive ways.

How it evolves

A child is the organ’s recipe with a few single changes: a rule switched on or off, or a rule’s measurement, threshold, side or weight changed. The best child replaces the champion only if it scores better. The evolver is blind: it refuses any data that still holds the held-out cases, and selects on slices carved from the training cases alone. How big a step to take is regulated, not fixed, by Rechenberg’s one-fifth success rule. A separate judge, the only code that reads the held-out cases, scores every kept step afterwards.

What happened

First design (one validation slice): it climbed on that slice on all four domains, and got worse on unseen cases on three of them. That is the winner’s curse: choose among 241 variants by one small slice, and you fit the slice. Second design (must win on all three folds of the training cases): better than the first everywhere, with real gains on water and crop. It still lost ground on the shop, whose unseen months differ from its training. In both designs, on data with no signal, nothing selection kept came anywhere near the gate.

What this means. Selection on its own is not safe: it can make an organ worse on the cases that matter while it gets better on the cases it sees. Self-improvement is safe here only because of organ 3’s gate. A new recipe is adopted only if it beats the current organ on the device’s own unseen cases, so the shop’s loss would never be adopted. The water and crop gains would.

08Organ 5 · Breeding and the card

An organism travels as a picture. Two organisms can have a child, and the child carries only what it can prove.

The card

A card is an ordinary 256 × 256 PNG you can post, send or print. The genome’s signed bytes are written into the colours of its top four rows. The rest is a face drawn from the genome itself, so the same genome always looks the same and two genomes look different. Opening a card checks every part of the file, reads the genome out of the pixels, and checks its seal before anything grows. A card whose face doesn’t match the seed it carries is refused, so a card can’t look like one organism and deliver another.

Breeding

Two genomes breed into a child that names both parents and takes the higher of their bars. Where both parents carry an organ, the child’s recipe crosses theirs rule by rule. The child is then grown on the breeder’s own device, and every organ that doesn’t go live there is dropped, with its reason. A child is never a promise: it is what grew.

09Organs 6 and 7 · Self-powering, and the self-model

Organ 6 — work done once

Growing and evolving organs keeps asking for the same work. A pool keeps what was done: each feature’s column is sorted once, and each organ grown from a recipe on the same data is kept. The rule that makes this safe: a recycled result is byte-for-byte what fresh computation gives. In the sealed run, evolution did 0.71% of the sorts it asked for, and 77% of the organism’s organ growths came from the pool. It ran about 6.5 times faster. No power meter was attached, so energy is not claimed; what was measured is work not repeated.

Organ 7 — it knows how likely it is to be right

For every answer, the organism predicts its own chance of being right. It uses two things it knows about itself: how far the case is from its own line, and how much the case resembles what it grew on. That self-model is refined seven times, at finer and finer resolution, each time starting from the average of its last two versions. It is judged on cases it never saw. It beat the base rate on all four organs and settled to a fixed point. Answering only where it rates itself 80% or more likely right lifted the shop’s accuracy from 79.9% to 93.0% on two-thirds of the cases. What is measured is calibration and convergence. That a self-model reaching a fixed point is awareness is not claimed.

10Organ 8 · The mesh

Organisms on their own devices, coupled into one web that wires, prunes and grows itself. No centre: every node is sovereign, and nothing passes between them except signed messages through the gate.

Advertise and route

Each node tells the others which organs it has live and how well each scored on cases it never saw. A task a node can’t answer goes to the node that can, and the answer comes back signed. In the sealed run the mesh answered 96.5% of 288 unseen cases. The same nodes alone would have answered 54.2%.

Grow what it lacks

A node that keeps having to send a domain away, while holding that domain’s data itself, asks for a lesson. It grows the recipe on its own data, and adopts it only if it clears the gate there. The clinic grew a triage specialist (AUC 0.951 on its own unseen cases) and the farm a shop one (0.815). The noisy node’s request was refused on its own data.

Trust outcomes, not claims

A node that over-claims, advertising 0.90 while really scoring 0.62, is found out by evidence. Its claim counts as four cases, and after that what it actually did decides. In the sealed test that sent 79% of late tasks to the honest node, against 0% under the first rule. It is less than the 80% predicted, and the reason is measured: old evidence fades, so the liar gets retried.

What took three tries, and what is still open. The first mesh run’s “the links concentrate” test was weak: by its last rounds only one full node was left alive. The second test’s premise failed: the faulty node wasn’t actually worse. The third found the over-claimer out, but not as completely as predicted. All three verdicts are kept below. The six nodes run in one process, so this measures the mesh’s logic and its signed messages, not a radio network. A run across real devices is the second of the three receipts.

11Organ 9 · The immune system

Every attack makes it stronger, and it never turns on itself. The gate already refuses anything not signed by a trusted key. This organ deals with what a gate alone cannot: a flood of forgeries, a stolen key, and a node taken over by someone else.

The rebound — attacks pay for themselves

A link the gate keeps refusing gets 16 refusals free. After that it must attach proof-of-work before its bytes are even looked at. The work it owes rises with every refusal and never falls. Refusing it costs this node one hash, and nothing is sent back. In the sealed run a flood of 5,040 forgeries cost organ 8 a signature check each. Organ 9 checked 400 (7.9%), and none in the flood’s last five rounds. The attacker burned 667,591 hashes buying looks, and every one was refused.

Proofs — herd immunity that cannot be faked

An honest node’s counter only goes up. So a key that signs two different messages on the same counter is in two hands, and the two signed messages are the proof. Every node rebuilds both and checks both signatures itself before it acts. A proof against an honest key cannot be made, so no node can be smeared. When farm’s key was stolen, north caught the clash. The other four nodes checked north’s proof and revoked the key, all in round 10, the round it was stolen. No task went to the thief.

The watch — behaviour, not identity

A key stolen outright leaves no clash to catch. What the new holder does instead is behave unlike the owner. Each node keeps a baseline of what each peer claims, how it otherwise conducts itself, and how much it sends. It cuts off a peer whose round departs further than honest rounds were measured to. The limits come from honest behaviour alone, so no attack is ever needed to train them. South was taken over in round 14 and began claiming 0.97 at everything. Every honest node cut it off that same round. It was sent 4 cases from then on, against 37 under organ 8.

After Gary W. Floyd, Lumiea Systems Research Division — ThunderStruck Service LLC — “GEP-Based Security: Behavioral Entropy Signatures for Cybersecurity and DevOps,” 2025 (open defensive prior art, CC BY 4.0): identity is not enough; behaviour is authoritative.

A memory that is not a way in

Bytes a node refused are remembered and refused on sight, in a memory that never grows past its cap of 256. Under the same flood organ 8’s uncapped memory reached 5,323, while organ 9’s peaked at 185. Behaviour judgements stay with the node that made them: another node cannot re-run them, so they are never spread.

What failed, and what was found. While building this organ we found that organ 8’s shared fingerprints protected nothing. Every message is addressed to one organism, so every other node’s gate already refused those bytes at its shape check, more cheaply than a hash. They are gone, and proofs replace them. The first watch failed: it kept one mix of everything a peer sent, and honest rounds swung as far as 0.8 on a handful of tasks. Its limit hit the ceiling of 1, so it could cut no one, and the takeover went uncaught. That is why organ 9’s own “no false cuts” pass is hollow. The second attempt (9b) keeps the channels apart, as Gary W. Floyd’s method does. The claims channel did the work there: honest rounds reached 0.5, set by a node growing a specialist, and the takeover reached 0.75. The conduct channel reached 1 on honest rounds, so it tells nothing apart on its own.
Revoking a stolen key also cuts off its real owner until it re-keys: farm’s own requests to the others went unanswered, and organ 9 answered 234 tasks right against organ 8’s 241. Under organ 8 the stolen key kept half-working for farm and the thief alike, racing each other on the counter. Our seal had said farm was lost under either organ; for organ 8 that was wrong. Finally, the mesh runs in one process with no real timing, so the “too regular” half of Gary W. Floyd’s method is not measured here. The attackers’ moves were fixed in advance; an outside adversary is the third receipt.

Opening a genome — verify before parse

  1. It starts ◊ life.v1 and is at most 2,048 bytes, or it is “not a genome” / “too large”.
  2. The last line is found by position and must be a 128-hex signature; the line before it, a 64-hex maker key. Nothing else has been read yet.
  3. If this device keeps a trust list, the maker must be on it.
  4. The signature is checked over the exact bytes of every line above it.
  5. Only now is the genome parsed — strictly — and written back out. It must come back byte-identical, so every genome has exactly one form and one id.

13For your business

Write the AI once. Sign it. Send the same few hundred bytes to every shop, clinic, plant or field device. Each one grows its own AI from its own records, checks it against its own past, and answers only where it has earned it. None of its records go anywhere.

What it does for each role

Chief executive

One signed recipe rolls out to every site, and each site’s AI is fitted to that site — not an average of all of them. You can show a regulator, an auditor or a customer exactly what it does: every rule is printed, and every answer comes with the rules that fired.

Finance

No licence fee: the code is MIT. This tier runs on machines you already own, with no model hosting and no per-call charges. What you ship to each site is a few hundred bytes. We don’t print a saving we can’t source. Put your own figures in the calculator below.

Operations

A site’s board shows every organ live, cut or dormant, and why. An organ that has not earned its place says “I don’t know” instead of mis-routing work. Rolling out means copying one small file.

Technology & security

Each genome’s signature (Ed25519) is checked before any of it is read, and devices can keep a list of the only makers they accept. In the sealed run, 486 of 486 one-character changes were refused. No data egress: 0 network calls while growing, and this page’s own security policy forbids connecting anywhere but its own site. Deterministic, so any audit can re-run it to the same fingerprint.

Data & analytics

Each organ is a sum of up to six single-measurement thresholds: readable, editable, testable. Every organ is graded on held-out cases against a bar written into the signed genome. The pre-registration tooling here — seal, measure, judge, order — is yours to reuse for your own models.

The people using it

An answer tells you what it decided, the score, the line it was judged against and the rules behind it. When it hasn’t earned the right to answer, it says so, so you know when to use your own judgement.

Security, compliance and data protection

  • Data stays put. Growing and answering run on the device; the records never leave it. That is data minimisation by construction. It is not a certification, so your own data-protection assessment still applies.
  • Signed, and checked first. A genome carries a digital signature (Ed25519, the scheme modern SSH keys use). Only the holder of the maker’s private key can produce it, and changing any character of the genome breaks it. A device refuses the genome before reading any of it unless the signature checks out over the exact bytes. A device can also keep a list of the only makers it accepts.
  • One form, one fingerprint. Every genome has exactly one byte form, and the same genome on the same data grows the same organism id anywhere. An auditor re-runs it and compares fingerprints.
  • Gated, not trusted. No organ answers unless it cleared the signed bar on cases it never grew on; the bar is part of what the maker signs.
  • Proven code. The two kernels are mutation-gated: every one of 69 deliberate faults was caught by the tests. CI re-derives the measurement byte for byte on every push.
  • This page. Its Content-Security-Policy pins its own scripts by hash and allows connections only to its own site — it cannot send what you type anywhere.
  • Nothing third-party inside. The package installs no dependencies. The only cryptography is the platform’s own WebCrypto. Every change re-runs the tests, the mutation gate and every sealed measurement on GitHub’s runners. A change to how organisms grow shows up as a different organism id, so it cannot slip through unseen.

Risks, answered straight

Is it accurate enough?

On the one real dataset — 12,330 online shop sessions — it ranks buyers above non-buyers with AUC 0.815 and is right 79.9% of the time on November–December sessions it never grew on. The three other domains here (field triage, drinking water, crop planting) are synthetic test domains, not real patients, samples or fields: 0.952, 0.748 and 0.754. Each organ is a small sum of rules, not a deep model; for some problems that is the ceiling.

How will it compare with what we run now?

Only your own data can tell, and we won’t guess. That is what step 5 of the roll-out does: the organism runs beside your current process on the same cases, and you compare its calls and its abstentions with what actually happened. Organ 2 on this page does the same against language models up to 14 billion parameters, sealed before they answered.

How does it keep up as the business changes?

Germinate again on newer records, which is one call that takes milliseconds. Every organ is re-graded on held-out cases each time. One that no longer clears the bar is cut and abstains, with no retraining project and nobody needing to notice first.

Will it behave the same in an urban store and a rural one?

Each store grows its own organs from its own records, so each is fitted to that store. On this page, two devices each holding half the history moved no organ’s AUC by more than 0.0054. Where a store’s data doesn’t support an organ, it is cut at that store and nowhere else.

What do we do if a maker key is stolen?

Rotate it. Make a new key, re-seal your genomes with it, and give your devices a trust list naming only the new key. From their next germination they refuse anything signed with the old one. Keep maker keys offline so this is rare. Organ 1 has no automatic revocation list; the rotation is a procedure you run.

Isn’t a gate of 0.6 a low bar?

It is a default. The bar is a field in the genome, signed by its maker. Set it as high as your question demands. The gate is a floor that has been tested at its edges: an organ exactly on the bar lives, and one a hair under it is cut.

What if a maker key is stolen?

Whoever holds it can sign genomes that devices trusting that maker will accept. Keep maker keys offline and use trust lists. Organ 1 has no revocation list yet, and we say so rather than imply one.

Does it learn and improve by itself?

Organ 1 grows fresh from whatever data the device holds each time it germinates; it does not improve itself. Self-improvement — variation kept only when it beats held-out cases, judged blind — is organ 4, and it will be measured when it lands.

Has anyone outside tried to break it?

Not yet. Inside: every one-character change, swapped keys and wrong makers were refused; the mutation gate caught 69 of 69 faults. An outside adversary is the last of the three receipts, and it is not claimed until it happens.

14White paper

The Living Seed, organ 1: a signed genome and offline germination of gated, abstaining organs — a pre-registered measurement.

Abstract

We describe a genome format of at most 2,048 bytes that carries recipes for up to eight predictive organs, a held-out bar, a lineage and an Ed25519 seal, and an operator Γ that grows the genome into an organism on a device’s own data without the network. A genome is verified before it is parsed and has a single canonical byte form. Each organ’s structure is carried in the genome; its thresholds are fitted to the device’s training cases. It is then graded on held-out cases against the signed bar: an organ that clears it answers, and every other organ abstains. Nine rules were sealed with the hash of every measuring file before measurement, two of them predictions. All nine held. A 496-byte, four-organ genome reproduced its source library’s held-out AUCs exactly (0.815, 0.952, 0.748, 0.754) and grew offline with 0 network calls. All 486 single-character tamperings were refused. Halving a device’s history moved no organ’s AUC by more than 0.0054. With the shop labels shuffled, that organ scored 0.513 and was cut while the other three answered every case.

1 · The problem

AI today ships as large trained artefacts or as services that the data must travel to. Both are opaque, and neither is fitted to the place it runs. Neither declines to answer outside what it has shown it can do. For a business with many sites, that means shipping either gigabytes or records, and trusting answers it cannot inspect.

2 · The genome

A genome is nine or more lines of text: a tag and name, a parents line, a gate line, one line per organ, the maker’s key and a signature over everything above it. Each organ line is a seed-library seed: the organ’s domain, a seed number, a packed recipe of up to six single-measurement rules (feature, quantile, side, weight) in 10 bytes, and a library check code. The check code catches corruption but not forgery, since every ribosome holds its key. The genome’s signature is what stops forgery. Opening is verify before parse: the signature and key are located by position and checked over the exact bytes before any field is read. The fields are then parsed strictly and re-rendered, and the result must be byte-identical, so a genome has exactly one form and one id (the first 16 hex characters of the SHA-256 of its signed body).

3 · Germination

For each organ, Γ looks for the domain’s data on the device. With none, the organ is dormant. Otherwise the ribosome sets each rule’s threshold at its quantile of that feature in the device’s training cases. It grades the resulting scorecard on held-out cases it never grew on, and fixes an operating point from the training cases alone. The organism is a plain record of the genome id, the maker, the substrate and each organ’s state, scores and rules. Its id is the SHA-256 of its canonical JSON, so the same genome on the same data grows the same organism on any engine.

4 · The gate and abstention

An organ goes live only when its held-out AUC is at least the genome’s bar. Asked about a case, a live organ returns VERIFIED with its label, score, threshold and the rules that fired. A cut or dormant organ returns ABSTAIN with the reason, and so does an organ asked about a case that lacks a measurement its rules read. We gate on AUC rather than accuracy because accuracy rewards a majority-class guess. The organ cut on shuffled labels would still have been 70.5% accurate.

5 · Method

Before measuring, tools/seal.mjs wrote data/prereg.json: the nine rules, which were properties and which predictions, what had already been seen during development, and the SHA-256 of the 17 files that would measure. It was committed first. tools/measure.mjs then produced a deterministic record using seeded maker keys and no clock. tools/judge.mjs, one of the pinned files, judged it, and tools/order.mjs proves from git that the seal’s last commit is a strict ancestor of the record’s first, on byte-identical pinned files. Most rules were seen during development and are claimed as properties of the build. Only R6 (half the history suffices, within 0.03 AUC) and R8 (no signal reads as a coin, and the rest stays whole) were unseen in the part that mattered.

7 · Limitations

  • Organs are small rule sums; the bar defaults to 0.6.
  • There is no maker-key revocation in organ 1.

  • There has been no outside adversary yet.
  • The genome is readable text, not further compressed.

Then the whole organism, and the three receipts.

What we do not claim

The Living Seed’s specification includes a self-model organ whose self-prediction is to converge to a fixed point. We will build and measure that mechanism. We do not claim that it is, or will produce, consciousness, and we do not claim that its self-improvement is open-ended. Both are kept as questions the instruments exist to investigate, not results.

15Questions

Is this a language model?

No. Nothing that runs in germination or in an answer is a language model. Each organ is a sum of readable threshold rules.

Where does the data on this page come from?

From this page’s own files, loaded once and cached for offline use: the UCI Online Shoppers Purchasing Intention dataset (real), and seed-library’s three synthetic domains, generated in your browser. Nothing is fetched while it grows.

Can I use my own data?

Yes, in the code: a substrate is any set of datasets in the seed-library shape (rows, features, labels, train and held-out cases). This page uses the four built-in domains so the results can be checked against the committed record.

Why is the demo maker key public?

So that anyone can reproduce the seal byte for byte. Ed25519 signatures are deterministic. A real maker keeps a real key secret and offline. “Make your own genome” shows that: your browser makes a fresh key that never leaves the tab.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After the first visit a service worker keeps this page’s files, and germination itself never uses the network.
